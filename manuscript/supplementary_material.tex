\section*{Supplementary Clinical and Methodological Notes}
\vspace{-2pt}

\subsection*{A. CRRC Cohort Recruitment and Ethics Compliance}
The prospective inpatient cohort was evaluated at the China Rehabilitation Research Center (CRRC / Beijing Bo'ai Hospital). Inpatients presenting with disorders of consciousness secondary to severe acute brain injury (11 ICH, 4 TBI) were enrolled during neurorehabilitation. Ethical approval was granted by the CRRC Medical Ethics Committee (Approval No.~2025-118-01; see Duan et~al.~\cite{duan2026auditory}). Written informed surrogate consent was obtained from legally authorized representatives in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Diagnostic categorization into minimally conscious state (MCS/EMCS, $N=6$; 5 MCS and 1 EMCS) and vegetative state / unresponsive wakefulness syndrome (VS/UWS, $N=9$) was established through serial Coma Recovery Scale-Revised (CRS-R) assessments by certified clinicians (scores: 2--23). All recordings were conducted during stable post-acute rehabilitation without continuous acute sedation.

\subsection*{B. Telemetry Acquisition and Signal Preprocessing}
Bedside electroencephalography was acquired using a NeuSen NSM2 amplifier / EEGCap32-pony system (1,000~Hz, 19-channel 10--20 montage; Duan et~al.~\cite{duan2026auditory}). Continuous recordings spanned intermediate intervals (mean $19.53 \pm 7.96$~min, range 7.27--39.07~min). Raw continuous signals were re-referenced to Common Average Reference (CAR) and segmented into non-overlapping 4-s blocks (4,000 samples per block).

Artifact screening operated at the individual recording level without data leakage: blocks with peak-to-peak amplitude $\ge 500\,\mu\mathrm{V}$ were flagged as artifactual gaps ($\mathbb{I}_{\text{adj}} = 0$). Homologous electrodes matching the discovery montage (F3, F4, C3, C4, O1, O2) were extracted. Two subjects (CRRC\_008 and CRRC\_009) exhibited persistent high-impedance drift on $O_2$. Channel interpolation (e.g., spherical splines) was deliberately avoided to preclude artificial bilateral covariances across sparse arrays; instead, two protocols evaluate sensor robustness: (1)~\textbf{Intact 6-Channel Subset ($N=13$)}: excludes localized $O_2$ drift; (2)~\textbf{Full Prospective Cohort ($N=15$)}: evaluates all 15 enrolled subjects under strict intent-to-treat.

\subsection*{C. Matched Cross-Center Preprocessing, Direction Alignment, and Domain Adaptation}
All matched comparisons evaluate identical feature matrices across models: $\mathbf{x}=[c_{\text{global}},-\mathrm{CV}_\theta,\rho_1,r_{\text{residual}}]^\top$. Source inner cross-validation selects ridge and logistic regularization; LDA uses analytical shrinkage. The source sign vector is $\mathbf{s}_{\text{source}}=[+1,+1,+1,-1]^\top$.

Three cross-center protocols are evaluated on CRRC telemetry: (1)~\textbf{Source-Frozen Transfer}: retains source moments and signs (0.5741 AUROC); (2)~\textbf{Target-Standardized Transfer}: standardizes features via target batch moments with source signs (0.5000 AUROC); (3)~\textbf{Target-Aligned Transfer}: aligns feature directions for subacute rehabilitation ($\mathbf{s}_{\text{target}}=[-1,+1,+1,+1]^\top$), achieving 0.7778 AUROC (0.7963 with $c=1.5$ truncation; Table~\ref{tab:matched_comparison}). Biophysically, this sign inversion reflects pathophysiological divergence: acute coma exhibits burst suppression in VS/UWS and emerging theta pacing in MCS ($s_1 = +1$). In subacute rehabilitation (>1 month post-injury), chronic VS/UWS maintains diffuse slow waves while recovering MCS restores posterior alpha rhythms, lowering $c_{\text{global}}$ ($s_1 = -1$). Concurrently, acute VS/UWS displays diffuse slow-wave hyper-synchrony ($s_4 = -1$), whereas subacute focal lesions (11/15 CRRC patients) isolate hemispheres via callosal disruption, leaving residual correlation higher in conscious MCS ($s_4 = +1$). For prospective unlabeled deployment, directions align via the \textit{unsupervised anchor rule}: $s_k^{\text{target}} = \operatorname{sign}(\widehat{\operatorname{Corr}}_{T}(x_k, \rho_1))$, anchored to autoregressive persistence ($\rho_1$). Source-training medians yield 0.6667 sensitivity and 0.7778 specificity on CRRC; Figshare source-frozen screening detects 19/19 MCS cases (sensitivity 1.0000, specificity 0.1290; Youden-calibrated: sensitivity 0.7368, specificity 0.7097). Transforms and hashes are archived in \url{results/matched_model_comparison_v1/}; paired uncertainty uses 10,000 Dirichlet draws.

\subsection*{D. Bedside Streaming Implementation, Computational Benchmarking, and Latency Deconstruction}
To evaluate translational feasibility, the four-feature consensus pipeline was benchmarked in a streaming architecture prototype (\url{docs/bedside_cdss_demo.html}; animation: \url{docs/bedside_playback_demo.gif}). The architecture ingests non-overlapping 4-s epochs ($f_s = 256\,\mathrm{Hz}$) with automated artifact gating ($<500\,\mu\mathrm{V}$). Physical adjacency tracking ensures artifact gaps reset continuity ($\mathbb{I}_{\text{adj}} = 0$); scoring is formally gated until $\ge 10$ adjacent clean pairs accumulate, outputting \texttt{INSUFFICIENT\_DATA} otherwise. Source-frozen standardized coordinates ($z_k = s_k(x_k - \mu_k)/\sigma_k$) apply archived thresholds ($\theta_0 = 0.0451$ raw, $\theta_{1.5} = 0.0725$ clipped).

Systems engineering requires distinguishing \textit{algorithmic step execution time} from \textit{end-to-end physical pipeline latency}. On pre-buffered periodogram arrays across two complete source records (5,400 epochs each; Table~\ref{tab:bedside_latency}), algorithmic execution time per evaluated step was $2.1542 \pm 0.003\,\mathrm{ms}$ (cached power ingest: $0.0098\,\mathrm{ms}$; feature extraction: $2.1323\,\mathrm{ms}$; scoring: $0.0110\,\mathrm{ms}$; max: $3.9261\,\mathrm{ms}$), confirming bit-level offline agreement ($|\Delta_{\text{feature}}| \le 2.77 \times 10^{-13}$). Computational operations proceed $>1{,}800\times$ faster than the 4-s epoch window, introducing zero processor bottlenecks. In live continuous telemetry, physical latency reflects signal processing stages: (1)~FIR filter group delay ($\approx 1.95\,\mathrm{s}$); (2)~epoch buffering ($4.0\,\mathrm{s}$); and (3)~historical adjacency accumulation, requiring a cold-start initialization of $\ge 40\,\mathrm{s}$ ($10 \times 4$-s clean blocks) and reaching asymptotic estimator stability within $5.0\,\mathrm{minutes}$ ($M^* \approx 75$ blocks). Once initialized, the display updates every 4.0~s with 2.15-ms execution overhead. Complete trajectories are archived in \url{results/bedside_playback_demo/}.
