\begin{abstract}
Conventional bedside electroencephalography markers discard temporal sequence order, obscuring dynamic cortical reverberation critical for detecting covert consciousness. We formulate an interpretable electrophysiological framework combining physically adjacent lag-one autocorrelation, normalized signal jump rate, and common-mode-decoupled residual bilateral correlation under equal-weight consensus scoring. Evaluation spanned four cohorts totaling 129 patients across continuous telemetry, rapid triage, and circadian regimes. On continuous discovery telemetry from 41 patients, the primary four-feature suite attained an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve of 0.9324 across 25 cross-validation partitions, exceeding the strongest matched supervised baseline by a paired margin of 0.0360 (95 percent confidence interval 0.0090 to 0.0728). Physical sequence dependence was confirmed by 10,000 synchronous whole-channel temporal permutations, reducing discrimination from 0.8911 to a permutation baseline of 0.8182 plus or minus 0.0049. On external inpatient telemetry from 15 subacute patients at China Rehabilitation Research Center, source-frozen transfer yielded an area of 0.5741, whereas unsupervised persistence-anchored direction alignment reached 0.7778 (0.7963 with soft truncation), capturing injury-dependent bilateral decoupling. On 120-second triage records from 50 patients, the jump-rate operator achieved 0.7691, detecting all 19 minimally conscious cases. Sleep-stage transitions across 23 patients captured macro-scale circadian dynamics. Requiring 2.15 milliseconds of algorithmic execution time per evaluated update step, this parameter-free framework operates over 1,800 times faster than the 4-second telemetry window, supporting auditable bedside neuromonitoring.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{S}{evere} acute brain injury from trauma, anoxia, or stroke leaves patients in prolonged disorders of consciousness (DoC), clinically categorized into the vegetative state/unresponsive wakefulness syndrome (VS/UWS) and the minimally conscious state (MCS)~\cite{giacino2002minimally,giacino2018practice}. Distinguishing MCS from VS/UWS informs decisions on withdrawing life-sustaining treatment, administering analgesia, and projecting rehabilitation potential. Bedside clinical evaluations carry an estimated 41\% misdiagnosis rate~\cite{schnakers2009diagnostic}. Although standardized assessments such as the Coma Recovery Scale-Revised (CRS-R)~\cite{schnakers2009diagnostic} reduce diagnostic error, motor impairment and fluctuating arousal frequently confound behavioral examinations~\cite{cruse2011bedside}. Prospective multi-center cohorts establish that approximately 25\% (and 15\%--35\% across multimodal cohorts) of behaviorally unresponsive patients exhibit cognitive-motor dissociation (CMD)~\cite{bodien2024cognitive}, retaining volitional cortical responses without overt motor output.

Functional neuroimaging and perturbational protocols (fMRI, $^{18}\text{F}$-FDG PET, TMS-EEG)~\cite{casali2013theoretically,chennu2017brain} show that preserved cortical complexity differentiates MCS from VS/UWS, but transport hazards preclude routine ICU deployment. Continuous bedside EEG provides a practical modality for tracking recovery on sparse ICU montages (4--8 electrodes)~\cite{sitt2014large,engemann2018robust,bareham2020spectral}. Prior work examined non-linear entropy~\cite{bandt2002permutation,sitt2014large}, microstates~\cite{ling2024microstates}, sleep architecture~\cite{wielek2018sleep,xiao2026sleep}, detrended fluctuation analysis (DFA)~\cite{thiery2018long,wei2023aberrant}, source connectivity~\cite{bai2021spontaneous}, and criticality~\cite{toker2022consciousness}. Yet bedside translation remains limited: multi-scale DFA is disrupted by non-stationary artifact gaps, source imaging is ill-posed on sparse arrays, entropy is artifact-sensitive, and deep classifiers risk overfitting small cohorts ($N < 50$). Bedside assessment thus relies on band power, marginal dispersion ($-\mathrm{CV}_\theta$), and unadjusted bilateral correlations~\cite{bareham2020spectral,wielek2018sleep}, bounding discrimination to 0.75--0.82 AUROC.

\subsection{The Permutation Invariance Defect of Conventional Metrics}
This diagnostic plateau stems from a shared limitation: strict permutation invariance. Conventional pipelines rely on aggregate dispersion ($-\mathrm{CV}_\theta$) and instantaneous bilateral correlation ($r_{\text{interhem}}$), which are invariant under temporal sequence permutation. Treating continuous EEG trajectories as unordered sets discards directional multi-second continuity and cortical reverberation.

\subsection{The Mesocircuit Hypothesis and Autoregressive Memory}
This methodological gap diverges from established neurobiology of conscious recovery, specifically the anterior forebrain mesocircuit hypothesis~\cite{schiff2010recovery}. Under this framework, sustained awareness depends on recurrent excitatory loops linking the central thalamus, striatum, and frontoparietal association cortices. Preserved mesocircuit connectivity manifests electrophysiologically as multi-second autoregressive continuity, quantifiable via lag-1 autocorrelation ($\rho_1$) across consecutive epochs. In VS/UWS, widespread deafferentation and loss of striatal inhibition disinhibit the internal globus pallidus (GPi), driving central thalamic hyperpolarization and degrading cortical activity into memoryless fluctuations. Distinguishing conscious capacity from reflexive unresponsiveness thus requires operators sensitive to physical sequence persistence.

As illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:schematic}a, individual theta trajectories in MCS (e.g., P36) exhibit sustained multi-second wave-holding, whereas VS/UWS trajectories (e.g., P25) break into decoupled transients. Static dispersion ($-\mathrm{CV}_\theta$) displays substantial overlap between cohorts ($p = 0.0264$; Fig.~\ref{fig:schematic}c), whereas lag-1 persistence ($\rho_1$) reliably separates conscious from unresponsive states ($p = 0.0227$; Fig.~\ref{fig:schematic}b).

For short triage records ($< 5$~min) where sample autocorrelation suffers elevated variance ($\operatorname{Var}(\hat{\rho}_1) \sim \mathcal{O}(1/M)$), we formulate the normalized jump rate ($D_1$) across verified adjacent epochs ($\mathbb{I}_{\text{adj}} = 1$), stabilizing short-horizon temporal estimation while preventing spurious concatenation across artifact gaps (Section~II-C).

\subsection{Spurious Bilateral Coupling Driven by Diffuse Slow Waves}
In acute brain injury, diffuse delta/theta oscillations propagate via volume conduction and subcortical pacemaking. Although common average reference (CAR) removes linear potential offsets, rectified band-power envelopes retain shared variance ($R^2 \approx 47\%$), inflating raw inter-hemispheric correlation. Decoupling this common mode via ordinary least squares (OLS) orthogonal projection isolates residual bilateral resonance ($r_{\text{residual}}$; Section~II-D), distinguishing pathological slow-wave co-activation in VS/UWS from preserved modular dynamics in MCS.

\subsection{Contributions of This Work}
This study introduces an order-aware electrophysiological framework with three principal contributions:

1) \textit{Order-aware temporal operators and artifact decoupling}: We formulate lag-1 autocorrelation ($\rho_1$) for continuous telemetry alongside normalized adjacent jump rates ($D_1$) for short recordings under physical adjacency constraints ($\mathbb{I}_{\text{adj}} = 1$), analytically unified via $D_1 \approx 2\pi^{-1/2}(-\mathrm{CV}_\theta)(1-\rho_1)^{1/2}$. Synchronously shuffling 10,000 surrogate sequences degrades classification from 0.8911 AUROC to permutation baseline ($0.8182 \pm 0.0049$, $p < 0.0001$), confirming that diagnostic separation requires chronological sequence order rather than static marginal distributions. OLS orthogonal projection decouples shared slow-wave envelope variance ($R^2 \approx 47\%$) to isolate intrinsic bilateral resonance ($r_{\text{residual}}$).

2) \textit{Interpretable, parameter-free bedside consensus}: The parsimonious four-feature suite $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ortho}}=[c_{\text{global}},-\mathrm{CV}_\theta,\rho_1,r_{\text{residual}}]^\top$ attains 0.9324 AUROC and 0.8998 average precision (AP) on primary discovery telemetry. Given identical inputs, equal-weight consensus scoring exceeds the top matched supervised baseline (logistic regression) by $+0.0360$ AUROC (Dirichlet paired 95\% CI $[+0.0090, +0.0728]$), executing in 2.15~ms per update step under streaming telemetry ingestion (Supplementary Note~D).

3) \textit{Tiered multicenter evaluation across clinical regimes}: The framework is evaluated across 129 records spanning four cohorts structured into three clinical tiers: Tier 1 inpatient telemetry (CRRC, $N=15$) evaluates cross-hardware transfer and etiology-dependent spatial dynamics (0.5741 frozen vs. 0.7778--0.7963 under unsupervised anchor alignment); Tier 2 rapid triage (Figshare, $N=50$) demonstrates short-epoch jump dynamics ($D_1$, 0.7691 AUROC); and Tier 3 polysomnography (Wielek, $N=23$) tracks macro-scale sleep stage transitions across 24-h circadian cycles.

\subsection{Clinical Cohorts and Ethical Compliance}
Four multicenter cohorts totaling $N=129$ records were evaluated under institutional ethical approvals:

\textbf{1) Mendeley Discovery Cohort} ($N = 41$): Continuous 6-channel bedside EEG (F3, F4, C3, C4, O1, O2; 256~Hz) from 41 patients with severe brain injuries at FRCCICMR Moscow~\cite{nekrasova2020psg}: 12 MCS (8 $\text{MCS}^+$, 4 $\text{MCS}^-$; duration $6.00 \pm 0.00$~h) and 29 VS/UWS ($5.97 \pm 0.61$~h), diagnosed through repeated CRS-R assessments.

\textbf{2) CRRC Inpatient Cohort} ($N = 15$): Prospective bedside telemetry from 15 subacute male patients (6 MCS, 9 VS/UWS; 11 ICH, 4 TBI; age $51.7 \pm 12.3$~years, range $28\text{--}69$; duration $19.5 \pm 8.0$~min; Ethics No.~2025-118-01~\cite{duan2026auditory}) at China Rehabilitation Research Center (CRRC / Beijing Bo'ai Hospital) using a 32-channel NeuSen NSM2 system (1000~Hz, 4-s blocks; Supplementary Note~A). Homologous 6 channels were evaluated; two patients (CRRC\_008, CRRC\_009) exhibited localized mechanical drift at $O_2$, enabling assessment on both the full cohort ($N = 15$) and the drift-free subset ($N = 13$; 6 MCS including 1 EMCS, 7 VS/UWS).

\textbf{3) Figshare Short-Record Analysis} ($N = 50$): High-density resting-state EEG (250~Hz, 120-s records) from 50 patients with prolonged DoC (19 MCS, 31 VS/UWS) at Hangzhou Mingzhou Brain Rehabilitation Hospital~\cite{ling2024microstates,xu2021neural}, evaluating rapid bedside triage using homologous channels under fixed short-duration recordings.

\textbf{4) Wielek PSG Cohort} ($N = 23$): 24-hour polysomnography from 23 post-comatose patients (11 MCS, 12 VS/UWS)~\cite{wielek2018sleep}, with 30-second hypnograms (Wake, N1, N2, N3, REM) tracking macro-scale transition continuity across circadian sleep cycles.
\subsection{EEG Acquisition, Preprocessing, and Physical Adjacency Constraint}
\input{table1_results.tex}
\label{sec:preprocessing}
Continuous 6-channel EEG (256~Hz) was re-referenced to Common Average Reference (CAR) and segmented into non-overlapping 4-second epochs (1,024 samples). Spectral densities were computed across each epoch via Hann-tapered periodograms ($\Delta f = 0.25$~Hz), extracting theta (4--8~Hz) and alpha (8--13~Hz) band power.

Artifact screening at $500\,\mu\mathrm{V}$ retained $5{,}185.5 \pm 595.6$ blocks per patient ($96.98\% \pm 3.2\%$, $5.76 \pm 0.66$~h; rejection $2.84\% \pm 2.1\%$ in MCS vs. $3.09\% \pm 3.6\%$ in VS/UWS, $p=0.82$, Mann--Whitney $U$ test). A conservative $150\,\mu\mathrm{V}$ cutoff retained $90.14\% \pm 17.87\%$ blocks ($4{,}672.9 \pm 1{,}024.7$ pairs; minimum 668), confirming balanced retention.

Let $\mathcal{T} = \{t_1, t_2, \dots, t_N\}$ denote clean epoch indices in a continuous session, and let $G_{t_i}$ denote spatial mean theta power across all 6 channels at epoch $t_i$. To prevent spurious concatenation across artifact gaps, the physical adjacency indicator is defined as:
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{I}_{\text{adj}}(t_i, t_{i+1}) = 
\begin{cases}
1, & \text{if } t_{i+1} - t_i = 1, \\
0, & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
where 0 denotes an artifact gap, and $\mathbb{I}_{\text{adj}} = 1$ defines the adjacent index set $\mathcal{I}_{\text{adj}} = \{i \mid t_{i+1}-t_i=1\}$. Sequential metrics operate strictly on $\mathcal{I}_{\text{adj}}$, retaining $M = |\mathcal{I}_{\text{adj}}| = 5{,}133.3 \pm 687.7$ adjacent pairs per patient (minimum 1,678, validity threshold $M \ge 10$).
\subsection{Canonical Static Features and Order-Aware Dynamics}
Four baseline features define $\mathbf{x}_{\text{canonical}} = [c_{\text{global}}, \allowbreak c_{\text{spatial}}, \allowbreak -\mathrm{CV}_\theta, \allowbreak r_{\text{interhem}}]^\top \allowbreak \in \mathbb{R}^4$. We evaluate three expanded feature spaces: the 5-feature temporal extension $\mathbf{x}_{+\rho_1} = [c_{\text{global}}, c_{\text{spatial}}, -\mathrm{CV}_\theta, r_{\text{interhem}}, \rho_1]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^5$; the 5-feature combined suite $\mathbf{x}_{\text{combined}} = [c_{\text{global}}, c_{\text{spatial}}, -\mathrm{CV}_\theta, \rho_1, r_{\text{residual}}]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^5$; and the primary parsimonious 4-feature deployment suite $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ortho}} = [c_{\text{global}}, -\mathrm{CV}_\theta, \rho_1, r_{\text{residual}}]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^4$. Theta power (4--8~Hz) is the primary carrier because thalamocortical mesocircuit projections drive frontoparietal dynamics within this band~\cite{schiff2010recovery}, whereas delta reflects non-specific slowing and alpha is largely abolished in VS/UWS. Let $P_{\theta, t}^{(k)}$ and $P_{\alpha, t}^{(k)}$ denote theta and alpha powers of channel $k \in \{1, \dots, 6\}$ at clean epoch $t \in \{1, \dots, N\}$.

\textbf{1) Global Spectral Contrast ($c_{\text{global}}$)}: Quantifies the ratio of slow to fast power across all channels: $c_{\text{global}} = (\bar{P}_\theta - \bar{P}_\alpha)/(\bar{P}_\theta + \bar{P}_\alpha + \epsilon)$, where $\bar{P}_\theta = \frac{1}{6N}\sum_{k, t} P_{\theta, t}^{(k)}$, $\bar{P}_\alpha = \frac{1}{6N}\sum_{k, t} P_{\alpha, t}^{(k)}$, and $\epsilon = 10^{-12}$.

\textbf{2) Spatial Contrast ($c_{\text{spatial}}$)}: Computes mean slow-to-fast ratio: $c_{\text{spatial}} = \frac{1}{6}\sum_{k=1}^6 (\bar{P}_\theta^{(k)} - \bar{P}_\alpha^{(k)})/(\bar{P}_\theta^{(k)} + \bar{P}_\alpha^{(k)} + \epsilon)$. Fixed feature configurations were compared to assess spectral redundancy; the four-feature suite omitted spatial contrast (Section~III-C).

\textbf{3) Negative Theta Dispersion ($-\mathrm{CV}_\theta$)}: Measures temporal variance of mean theta $G_t = \frac{1}{6}\sum_{k=1}^6 P_{\theta, t}^{(k)}$: $-\mathrm{CV}_\theta = - \sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N (G_t - \bar{G})^2}/(\bar{G} + \epsilon)$, with temporal mean $\bar{G} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N G_t$.

\vspace{2pt}
\textbf{4) Inter-Hemispheric Bilateral Correlation ($r_{\text{interhem}}$)}: Averages raw pairwise Pearson correlations across symmetric bilateral pairs: $r_{\text{interhem}} = \frac{1}{3}\sum_{(L, R)} \operatorname{Corr}(\mathbf{y}_L, \mathbf{y}_R)$, where $\mathbf{y}_L, \mathbf{y}_R \in \mathbb{R}^N$ denote the 4-second block theta-power vectors across symmetric pairs $(L, R) \in \{(\text{F3}, \text{F4}), (\text{C3}, \text{C4}), (\text{O1}, \text{O2})\}$.

To capture multi-second sequential continuity, two order-aware temporal representations are formulated on physically adjacent blocks:

\textbf{Lag-1 Memory Persistence ($\rho_1$)}: Quantifies autoregressive temporal persistence across consecutive 4-second blocks:
\begin{equation}
\rho_1 = \operatorname{Corr}(\{G_{t_i}\}_{i \in \mathcal{I}_{\text{adj}}}, \{G_{t_{i+1}}\}_{i \in \mathcal{I}_{\text{adj}}}).
\end{equation}
High $\rho_1$ values reflect smooth baseline preservation, whereas low values signify rapid temporal decorrelation.

\textbf{Normalized Adjacent Jump Rate ($D_1$)}: Measures resistance to high-frequency baseline discontinuities:
\begin{equation}
D_1 = - \frac{1}{M}\sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}_{\text{adj}}} |G_{t_{i+1}} - G_{t_i}| / (\bar{G} + \epsilon),
\end{equation}
where $\bar{G} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N G_t$, $M = \sum \mathbb{I}_{\text{adj}}$, and $\epsilon = 10^{-12}$. Under stationary Gaussian increments $\Delta G_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 2\sigma^2(1 - \rho_1))$, expected jump magnitude satisfies $\mathbb{E}[|\Delta G_t|] = 2\pi^{-1/2} \sigma (1 - \rho_1)^{1/2}$, yielding $D_1 \approx 2\pi^{-1/2} (-\mathrm{CV}_\theta) (1 - \rho_1)^{1/2}$. Evaluating $D_1$ on log-power increments accounts for 93.91\% of empirical variance under this identity (Section~\ref{sec:results_ablation}). Given estimator variance $\operatorname{Var}(\hat{\rho}_1) \sim \mathcal{O}(1/M)$, $D_1$ provides a low-variance proxy for brief records ($M < M^* \approx 75$ epochs), whereas $\rho_1$ achieves asymptotic efficiency.

To test macro-scale persistence on the Wielek PSG cohort ($N=23$), stage stay persistence on 30-s hypnograms $\{s_t\}_{t=1}^L \in \{\text{Wake, N1, N2, N3, REM}\}$ is $P_{\text{stay}} = \frac{1}{L-1}\sum_{t=1}^{L-1} \mathbb{I}(s_{t+1} = s_t)$, with transition flexibility $T_{\text{trans}} = 1 - P_{\text{stay}}$.
 
These four features span spectral slowing, marginal dispersion, autoregressive continuity, and residual bilateral coupling, forming the primary deployment suite $[c_{\text{global}}, -\mathrm{CV}_\theta, \rho_1, r_{\text{residual}}]$.

\subsection{Residual Bilateral Resonance ($r_{\text{residual}}$)}
\input{table2_multiscale_hyperparam.tex}
For each symmetric bilateral pair $(L, R) \in \{(\text{F3}, \text{F4}), (\text{C3}, \text{C4}), (\text{O1}, \text{O2})\}$ with block theta-power vectors $\mathbf{y}_L, \mathbf{y}_R \in \mathbb{R}^N$, the nuisance signal across the remaining 4 channels is $z_t^{(L, R)} = \frac{1}{4}\sum_{k \notin \{L, R\}} P_{\theta, t}^{(k)}$. With standardized $\tilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(L, R)} \in \mathbb{R}^N$ and design matrix $\mathbf{X}_c^{(L, R)} = [\mathbf{1}, \tilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(L, R)}] \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times 2}$, the exact unregularized OLS projection operator $\mathbf{P}_{(L, R)}^\perp = \mathbf{I}_N - \mathbf{X}_c^{(L, R)} (\mathbf{X}_c^{(L, R)})^+ \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N}$ (with $\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{P}^\perp) = N - \operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{X}_c)$, where $\mathbf{X}_c^+$ denotes the Moore--Penrose pseudoinverse) computes orthogonalized residuals $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_L^{(L, R)} = \mathbf{P}_{(L, R)}^\perp \mathbf{y}_L$ and $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_R^{(L, R)} = \mathbf{P}_{(L, R)}^\perp \mathbf{y}_R$. The residual bilateral resonance averages pairwise correlations:
\begin{equation}
r_{\text{residual}} = \frac{1}{3} \sum_{(L, R)} \operatorname{Corr}(\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_L^{(L, R)}, \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_R^{(L, R)}).
\end{equation}
removing shared slow-wave envelope variance ($\approx 47\%$) to isolate intrinsic homologous coupling.

\subsection{Consensus Operators and Machine Learning Baselines}
\textbf{Linear Standardized Aggregation (Linear)}:
Equal-weight consensus follows the fixed-coefficient formulation of Dawes improper linear models~\cite{dawes1979robust}. The score is $S_{\text{linear}}(\mathbf{x})=K^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{K}s_kz_k$, where $z_k=(x_k-\mu_k^{\text{tr}})/\sigma_k^{\text{tr}}$ and each sign $s_k$ is fitted from the feature--label correlation in the source training partition. Standardization and signs are re-estimated inside every inner and outer training fold. For the full-source four-feature fit, the signs are $[+1,+1,+1,-1]^\top$. CRRC target alignment uses the unsupervised persistence-anchored vector $[-1,+1,+1,+1]^\top$ across models in common oriented coordinates (Supplementary Note~C). A soft-truncated variant clips coordinates at $c=1.5$ ($K^{-1}\sum_k\operatorname{clip}(s_kz_k,-c,c)$); the primary model uses $c=\infty$.

\textbf{Non-Parametric Rank Consensus ($S_{\text{rank}}$)}:
Adapted from multi-criteria decision frameworks~\cite{grabisch2009aggregation}, rank consensus maps signed features to empirical cumulative distribution function (CDF) midranks:
\begin{equation}
S_{\text{rank}}(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=1}^K \tilde{F}_k(s_k x_k),
\end{equation}
where $\tilde{F}_k(u) = \frac{1}{N_{\text{tr}}} \sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{tr}}} [ \mathbb{I}(s_k x_{i, k}^{\text{tr}} < u) + \frac{1}{2} \mathbb{I}(s_k x_{i, k}^{\text{tr}} = u) ]$. Curvature is fixed to $p = 1.0$ (mean on ranks), ensuring constant marginal substitution without uncalibrated parameters on small cohorts.

\textbf{Adaptive Fluctuation-Gated Hybrid (T-GECF)}:
To combine linear sensitivity with rank stability under sensor disturbances, T-GECF couples the two estimators via sample-level volatility gating. For signed standardized features $\tilde{x}_k = s_k (x_k - \mu_k^{\text{tr}})/(\sigma_k^{\text{tr}} + \epsilon)$, sample volatility is $v(\mathbf{x}) = \sqrt{\frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=1}^K (\tilde{x}_k - \bar{\tilde{x}})^2}$, standardized to $z_v(\mathbf{x}) = (v(\mathbf{x}) - \mu_v^{\text{tr}})/(\sigma_v^{\text{tr}} + \epsilon)$ against training folds. A continuous Sigmoid gate $\beta(\mathbf{x}) = 1 / (1 + \exp(-\kappa(z_v(\mathbf{x}) - \theta_0)))$ ($\theta_0 = 1.645, \kappa = 2.0$) mixes standardized rank and linear scores:
\begin{equation}
S_{\text{T-GECF}}(\mathbf{x}) = \beta(\mathbf{x}) \tilde{S}_{\text{rank}}(\mathbf{x}) + (1 - \beta(\mathbf{x})) \tilde{S}_{\text{linear}}(\mathbf{x}),
\end{equation}
where $\tilde{S}_{\text{rank}}$ and $\tilde{S}_{\text{linear}}$ are standardized to zero mean and unit variance using outer fold statistics prior to mixing. Elevated volatility ($z_v(\mathbf{x}) > 1.645$) dynamically shunts decision weight toward bounded rank consensus $\tilde{S}_{\text{rank}}(\mathbf{x})$, mitigating the influence of isolated channel artifacts or amplitude spikes.

\textbf{Supervised Machine Learning and Domain Baselines}:
Ridge, logistic regression, and shrinkage LDA receive exactly the same feature columns, standardized coordinates, direction coding, and subject partitions as equal-weight scoring. Ridge $\alpha$ and logistic $C$ are selected from $\{10^{-3},10^{-2},10^{-1},1,10,10^2,10^3\}$ using source-only inner three-fold stratified cross-validation; transforms are fitted on each inner training fold. LDA uses analytical shrinkage (\texttt{auto})~\cite{ledoit2004well}. The same source-selected parameters are retained across external protocols. The primary matched comparison uses $\mathbf{x}_{\text{ortho}}$; the short-record branch uses $[c_{\text{global}},c_{\text{spatial}},D_1,r_{\text{interhem}}]^\top$ for every model. Lempel-Ziv complexity, spectral entropy, and an EEGNet-style CNN~\cite{lawhern2018eegnet} provide additional domain baselines under the recorded protocols.

\subsection{Subject-Level Validation Protocol and Decision Thresholds}
Discovery evaluation uses five random seeds and five stratified subject-level folds per seed, giving 25 frozen outer partitions. Source means, scales, signs, empirical ranks, and hyperparameters are fitted strictly within training partitions. AUROC and average precision (AP) are averaged across outer folds; sensitivity, specificity, balanced accuracy, and F1 use pooled outer-test decisions, with each subject contributing once per seed. Each model's operating threshold is its source-training score median. Paired 95\% intervals use 10,000 subject-level Dirichlet Bayesian bootstrap draws~\cite{rubin1981bayesian}, sharing each subject's weight across folds and methods conditional on the fitted predictions. External validation separates frozen-source transfer, unlabeled target-batch standardization, and CRRC target direction alignment. Models share the same target transform within each protocol, remaining blinded to external labels until final metric evaluation. All reported classification operating points use source-training medians. Full parameters, transforms, thresholds, and predictions are archived with the evaluation scripts. A multivariable logistic regression on standardized $[c_{\text{global}}, R^2, \rho_1]$ provides the conditional association analysis.

\section{Experimental Results}

\subsection{The $2 \times 2$ Orthogonal Factorial Decomposition}

Table~\ref{tab:main_results} summarizes classification performance. In the $2 \times 2$ factorial decomposition (Fig.~\ref{fig:ablations}a), Cell A (Static, Linear) achieves 0.8200 AUROC, Cell B (Static, Rank) yields 0.7893, Cell C (Plus $\rho_1$, Linear) attains 0.8911, and Cell D (Plus $\rho_1$, Rank) reaches 0.8591. The temporal representation gain is $\Delta_{\text{rep}} = \operatorname{AUC}(\text{Cell C}) - \operatorname{AUC}(\text{Cell A}) = +0.0711$ (95\% CI $[+0.0011, +0.1534]$, positive gain probability 97.74\%), while $\Delta_{\text{fuse}} = \operatorname{AUC}(\text{Cell D}) - \operatorname{AUC}(\text{Cell C}) = -0.0320$ reflects the trade-off between linear resolution and bounded rank stability.

\subsection{Diagnostic Operating Characteristics and Imbalanced Precision-Recall}
At the pre-specified training median threshold, incorporating $\rho_1$ elevated Balanced Accuracy from 0.7201 to 0.7997 and Sensitivity from 0.8333 to 0.9167 (Table~\ref{tab:main_results}). Combining lag-1 persistence with residual bilateral resonance ($\mathbf{x}_{\text{combined}}$) improved the five-feature extension: under soft truncation ($c=1.5\sigma$), linear aggregation achieved \textbf{0.9149} AUROC (PR-AUC = \textbf{0.8782}, Balanced Accuracy = \textbf{0.8399}, Sensitivity = 0.9833). In rank consensus, this suite elevated AUROC to \textbf{0.8949}, matching unconstrained linear aggregation within $0.0047$ (0.8996).

\subsection{Feature Ablation and Collinearity Redundancy Analysis}\label{sec:results_ablation}
Replacing $-\mathrm{CV}_\theta$ with $\rho_1$ in the 4-feature suite yielded 0.8851 AUROC, confirming that sequence memory supersedes marginal variance. Across 41 patients, MCS showed higher persistence ($0.450 \pm 0.250$ vs. $0.287 \pm 0.181$, $p = 0.0227$, AUROC = 0.7299; Fig.~\ref{fig:schematic}b,c) and lower jump rates ($D_1$: $-0.420 \pm 0.089$ vs. $-0.558 \pm 0.168$, $p = 0.0112$, AUROC = 0.7557). Log-power increments verify the analytical identity ($r = 0.9691, p = 2.59 \times 10^{-25}, R^2 = 93.91\%$), making $D_1$ a stable proxy below $M^* \approx 75$ epochs ($5$~min). Central sensorimotor channels (C3--C4) showed the highest univariate discrimination ($\rho_1$: $0.3996 \pm 0.1863$ vs. $0.2611 \pm 0.1631$, $p = 0.0132$, AUROC = 0.7500), exceeding frontal (0.6264) and occipital (0.6810) pairs.

Regressing out the global common mode ($r_{\text{residual}}$) improved AUROC from 0.8200 to 0.8433, accounting for $R^2 = 47.27\% \pm 18.43\%$ of raw bilateral variance uniformly across groups ($46.45\%$ in MCS vs. $47.61\%$ in VS/UWS; $p = 0.7418$). Whereas raw bilateral correlation was non-significant ($0.563 \pm 0.168$ in MCS vs. $0.661 \pm 0.146$ in VS/UWS, $p = 0.0605$, AUROC = 0.6897), residualization unmasked residual bilateral differentiation beyond the shared background power envelope ($0.233 \pm 0.080$ vs. $0.376 \pm 0.193$, $p = 0.0211$, AUROC = 0.7328), reflecting modularity in MCS versus hyper-synchrony in VS/UWS. Multivariable logistic regression controlling for $c_{\text{global}}$ ($z = -0.812, p = 0.417$) and common-mode $R^2$ ($z = 0.329, p = 0.742$) showed that $\rho_1$ provides significant independent diagnostic value ($z = 2.460, p = 0.0139$, adjusted OR = $4.260$, 95\% CI $[1.343, 13.512]$), confirming conditional independence.

Because $c_{\text{global}}$ and $c_{\text{spatial}}$ are strongly collinear ($r = 0.9813, p = 1.58 \times 10^{-29}$), omitting $c_{\text{spatial}}$ to form the 4-feature deployment suite $[c_{\text{global}}, -\mathrm{CV}_\theta, \rho_1, r_{\text{residual}}]$ (Table~\ref{tab:main_results}) raised linear AUROC to \textbf{0.9324} (PR-AUC = \textbf{0.8998}; Balanced Accuracy = 0.8101, 95\% CI $[+0.0174, +0.2186]$ vs. baseline, positive gain probability 99.16\%) and rank consensus to \textbf{0.9076} (95\% CI $[+0.0137, +0.1725]$, sensitivity 1.0000). Dropping $c_{\text{global}}$ reached 0.9289 in linear and 0.9082 in rank consensus, while the 5-feature truncated suite (0.9149) assessed bounded-influence scoring.



\subsection{Temporal-Order Dependency via 10,000 Synchronous Surrogates}
To verify that performance gains originate strictly from temporal sequence order rather than incidental distributional properties, 10,000 synchronous whole-montage permutation surrogates were evaluated across all 41 patients~\cite{theiler1992testing}. Freezing intact training scales ($\sigma_k^{\text{tr, intact}}$) prevents inverse noise amplification on attenuated shuffled variances. Disrupting chronological sequence alignment degraded macro AUROC across all temporal configurations (Fig.~\ref{fig:violin}): Plus Lag-1 (Linear) dropped from 0.8911 to $0.8182 \pm 0.0049$ (range: $[0.7902, 0.8369]$, empirical $p < 0.0001$, zero of 10,000 surrogates matching intact performance); Plus Lag-1 (Rank) fell from 0.8591 to $0.7732 \pm 0.0416$ (range: $[0.6620, 0.9098]$, empirical $p = 0.0178$); and Plus Jump ($D_1$) degraded from 0.8611 to $0.7783 \pm 0.0040$ ($p < 0.0001$).

\vspace{-3pt}
\subsection{Comparison with Supervised Machine Learning and Domain Baselines}
On identical 4-feature inputs (Table~\ref{tab:main_results}; Fig.~\ref{fig:ablations}b), equal-weight linear scoring achieved 0.9324 AUROC versus 0.8813 for source-tuned ridge ($+0.0511$), 0.8964 for logistic regression ($+0.0360$; paired 95\% CI $[+0.0090, +0.0728]$), and 0.8769 for shrinkage LDA ($+0.0556$). Domain baselines yielded 0.5136 AUROC for LZC, 0.6953 for spectral entropy, and 0.5702 for the EEGNet-style CNN (Supplementary Table~S2).

\vspace{-3pt}
\subsection{Adaptive Gated Consensus (T-GECF) and Noise Stress Testing}
On the complete representation $\mathbf{x}_{\text{combined}}$, continuous Sigmoid volatility gating in T-GECF ($\theta_0 = 1.645, \kappa = 2.0$) achieved an AUROC of \textbf{0.9116} (95\% CI $[+0.0013, +0.1927]$ vs. canonical baseline; positive gain probability 97.68\%) with 0.8399 balanced accuracy, 0.9833 sensitivity, 0.6966 specificity, and PR-AUC = \textbf{0.8669} (Table~\ref{tab:main_results}). On $\mathbf{x}_{+\rho_1}$, T-GECF similarly achieved 0.8828 AUROC ($+0.0237$ over standard rank consensus 0.8591).

To assess resilience against sensor disturbances, impulse spike stress tests introduced outlier perturbations ($\pm \delta\sigma$, $\delta \in \{0, 5, 10, 20\}$) into individual feature coordinates across testing partitions. Under extreme $20\sigma$ impulse corruption, unconstrained linear aggregation dropped from 0.8911 to 0.7404 ($-0.1507$). In contrast, T-GECF shifted weight toward bounded rank consensus, retaining an AUROC of 0.8518 ($+0.1114$ over linear aggregation) while maintaining 0.9116 AUROC under unperturbed conditions.

\vspace{-3pt}
\subsection{Multiscale Window Sensitivity and Longitudinal Reliability}
Evaluating observational windows (Table~\ref{tab:multiscale_sensitivity}-A) showed consistent linear discrimination across 2~s (0.8916), 4~s (0.8911), and 8~s (0.8711) partitions, with rank consensus similarly stable (0.8453--0.8591). Truncating recordings showed that a 30-minute record attained 0.8867 AUROC, close to full-record performance. Test-retest reliability across five 1-hour segments ($N=38$, 190 patient-hours) yielded $\text{ICC}(3,1) = 0.4857$ for $\rho_1$, $0.6433$ for $D_1$, and $0.4511$ for $r_{\text{residual}}$. Higher-order lags decayed monotonically ($\rho_1 = 0.450 \to \rho_2 = 0.401 \to \rho_3 = 0.389$ in MCS; $0.287 \to 0.252 \to 0.208$ in VS/UWS), reflecting multi-second persistence. Under adjacency masking $\mathbb{I}_{\text{adj}}$, 50-iteration Monte Carlo dropout maintained stability ($r = 0.9942 \pm 0.0030$ at 10\% dropout; $r = 0.9199 \pm 0.0308$ at 50\% loss), while tightening artifact rejection to $150\,\mu\mathrm{V}$ preserved 0.8702 AUROC (+0.1729 over unrejected baseline 0.6973; +0.0502 over unconstrained baseline 0.8200). Contiguous 30-min block removal ($r = 0.9840 \pm 0.0166$) and partition depths (10- to 100-fold CV, $0.8900$--$0.9002$) verified stability across analytical scales.

\vspace{-3pt}
\subsection{External Waveform Validation and Sleep Macro-Dynamics}
\textit{1) Rapid Bedside Triage (Figshare, $N=50$)}: On 120-s records, jump rates reached 0.7691 AUROC (0.7199 static; baselines 0.7674--0.7759; diff vs. ridge $-0.0068$; Supplementary Table~S2). At source thresholds, it provides a high-sensitivity funnel (1.0000 sensitivity, 19/19 MCS; 0.1290 specificity; Table~\ref{tab:multiscale_sensitivity}-B); Youden calibration recovers balanced accuracy (0.7233; sensitivity 0.7368, specificity 0.7097).
\textit{2) Cross-Hardware Inpatient Validation (CRRC, $N=15$)}: Frozen transfer yielded 0.5741 AUROC; direction alignment ($\mathbf{s}_{\text{target}} = [-1, +1, +1, +1]^\top$) reached 0.7778 (0.7963 with $c=1.5$ truncation; baselines 0.7407--0.7593; drift-free $N=13$: 0.7143; Supplementary Table~S2). At source thresholds, truncated scoring attained 0.6667 sensitivity, 0.7778 specificity.
\textit{3) Circadian Sleep Macro-Dynamics (Wielek, $N=23$)}: Polysomnography across 24-hour records (11 MCS, 12 VS/UWS) showed that temporal continuity extends to macro-scale sleep architecture: VS/UWS patients exhibited state trapping ($P_{\text{stay}} = 0.8361 \pm 0.1001$), whereas MCS retained transition flexibility ($T_{\text{trans}} = 0.261 \pm 0.099$ vs. $0.164 \pm 0.100$, one-sided $p=0.0302$, AUROC = 0.7348; nocturnal sleep $p=0.0392$, AUROC = 0.7576; Table~\ref{tab:multiscale_sensitivity}-B).

\vspace{-3pt}
\subsection{Hyperparameter Sensitivity and Gating Grid Evaluation}
Supplementary Table~S3-A reports systematic benchmarks across truncation thresholds $c$ and continuous Sigmoid gating parameters $(\theta_0, \kappa)$. Varying Winsorized soft truncation from $c = 1.0\sigma$ to $c = \infty$ produced stable AUROC within $[0.8958, 0.9149]$ (balanced accuracy $0.8046$--$0.8399$, sensitivity $0.9333$--$0.9833$), peaking at $c = 1.5\sigma$ ($0.9149$) before approaching unconstrained linear aggregation ($0.8996$).

Evaluating T-GECF across a $4 \times 4$ grid crossing Gaussian quantile thresholds $\theta_0 \in \{1.282, \allowbreak 1.645, \allowbreak 1.960, \allowbreak 2.326\}$ and slopes $\kappa \in \{0.5, \allowbreak 1.0, \allowbreak 2.0, \allowbreak 5.0\}$ showed bounded variation (Supplementary Table~S3-B). Across all 16 configurations, AUROC remained bounded within $0.8996$--$0.9220$, exceeding the canonical baseline (0.8200) by $+0.08$ to $+0.10$. At $\theta_0 = 2.326$ ($\kappa=5.0$), $\beta(\mathbf{x}) \to 0$, smoothly recovering linear aggregation ($0.8996$).

\vspace{-2pt}

\section{Discussion}
\vspace{-2pt}
\vspace{-2pt}
\subsection{Physiological Significance of Autoregressive Memory}
Under the mesocircuit hypothesis~\cite{schiff2010recovery}, thalamocortical loops sustain multi-second theta continuity in MCS, whereas deafferentation in VS/UWS degrades fluctuations into decoupled transients ($\rho_1 \to 0$). Because marginal dispersion ($-\mathrm{CV}_\theta$) conflates reverberation with noise, enforcing chronological continuity ($\mathbb{I}_{\text{adj}}$) provides an auditable marker of thalamocortical integrity. Across centers, temporal persistence ($\rho_1$ for continuous telemetry, $D_1$ for 120-s triage, and sleep-stage flexibility for 24-h circadian records) acts as a consistent physiological anchor, whereas spectral and spatial features reflect regime-dependent dynamics requiring contextual calibration.



\vspace{-3pt}
\subsection{Resolution of Non-Specific Bilateral Confounding and Direction Inversion}
Residual correlation ($r_{\text{residual}}$, AUROC 0.8433) removes common-mode activity ($47.27\% \pm 18.43\%$ of bilateral variance), unmasking acute VS/UWS hyper-synchrony ($0.376 \pm 0.193$) versus preserved MCS modularity ($0.233 \pm 0.080$, $p = 0.0211$). In subacute CRRC patients (11/15 hemorrhage), this polarity inverted ($0.308$ in MCS vs. $0.210$ in VS/UWS): callosal hemorrhage isolates hemispheres in VS/UWS, whereas recovering MCS restores interhemispheric coupling~\cite{schiff2010recovery,chennu2017brain}. Similarly, chronic VS/UWS exhibits diffuse slowing ($c_{\text{global}} \uparrow$) while recovering MCS restores posterior alpha ($c_{\text{global}} \downarrow$)~\cite{bareham2020spectral}, resolved via the unsupervised $\rho_1$-anchor rule ($\mathbf{s}_{\text{target}} = [-1, +1, +1, +1]^\top$; Supplementary Note~C). In dense arrays, 4-channel averaging generalizes to subspace projection ($\mathbf{P}^\perp = \mathbf{I} - \mathbf{Z}\mathbf{Z}^+$; 46.52\% Mode-1 variance, Table~\ref{tab:multiscale_sensitivity}-B).

\vspace{-3pt}
\subsection{Operator Dynamics: Rank Consensus vs. Linear Aggregation}
Matched internal benchmarks support fixed-weight aggregation: the linear suite retained an AUROC advantage over supervised models on identical inputs. External validation showed comparable performance across classifiers, indicating that representation and calibration govern transfer. Soft truncation increased full-cohort CRRC AUROC from 0.7778 to 0.7963 (discovery 0.9291 vs. 0.9324 unclipped), providing bounded-influence scoring. Adaptive gating via T-GECF complements this by combining linear resolution with rank stability under impulse disturbances.

\vspace{-4pt}
\subsection{Clinical Scope, Cognitive Motor Dissociation, and Multimodal Triage}
A primary clinical application is detecting covert consciousness in cognitive-motor dissociation (CMD), where CRS-R misses volitional responses in up to 25\% of unresponsive patients~\cite{bodien2024cognitive}. Continuous lag-1 persistence ($\rho_1$) provides an auditable physiological marker to prioritize candidates for confirmatory active fMRI/PET or TMS-EEG~\cite{cruse2011bedside,casali2013theoretically}. Under continuous telemetry (prevalence $12/41$), the median threshold reaches 0.9167 sensitivity and 0.7034 specificity, projecting 52.2\% fewer transfers ($\Delta_{\text{referral}}=1-[P_{\text{MCS}}\,\mathrm{Sens}+(1-P_{\text{MCS}})(1-\mathrm{Spec})]$; 48.6\%--61.0\% across 15\%--35\% ICU prevalence) than universal escalation. In 120-s triage (Figshare), source thresholds ensure zero missed MCS cases (sensitivity 1.0000), while Youden calibration balances referral reduction to 42.6\% (sensitivity 73.68\%, specificity 70.97\%).

\vspace{-4pt}
\vspace{-4pt}
\subsection{Comparison with Recent Advanced Paradigms (2024--2026)}
Recent dense-array and sensory paradigms report high DoC discrimination: microstates reach 0.97 AUC~\cite{ling2024microstates}, entropy CNNs reach 0.902 AUC~\cite{qu2026diagnosis}, multimodal pipelines achieve 92.4\% accuracy~\cite{zhang2025multimodal}, auditory features differentiate consciousness levels~\cite{duan2026auditory}, and source graphs yield 0.714--0.893 AUC~\cite{hao2024uncovering}.
Translating them to bedside surveillance faces obstacles: high-density arrays are motion-sensitive, acoustic tests require specialized hardware, and deep models risk overfitting ($N < 50$). Combining order-aware persistence ($\rho_1, D_1$) with common-mode decoupling ($r_{\text{residual}}$) enables 4--6 channel operation with 2.15~ms per-step execution time and robust multicenter utility (0.7778--0.7963 on CRRC; 0.7691 on Figshare).

\vspace{-4pt}
\subsection{Limitations}
Limitations include: (1) discovery in 41 patients (12 MCS), warranting prospective expansion; (2) external cohorts evaluate distinct regimes: subacute telemetry (CRRC) reflects etiology-dependent spatial dynamics, whereas 120-s records (Figshare) assess jump dynamics ($D_1$); (3) unmeasured covariates, such as continuous sedatives ($\text{GABA}_A$ agonists) and craniectomy defects, warrant standardized washout and impedance modeling; (4) streaming latency includes 4-s buffering and $\ge 40$-s accumulation, though per-step execution takes 2.15~ms; and (5) sample sizes limit subgroup resolution across etiologies.

\vspace{-6pt}

\section{Conclusion}
Order-aware temporal descriptors and common-mode-adjusted bilateral dynamics provide a compact EEG representation for bedside consciousness assessment. On identical four-feature inputs, equal-weight consensus achieved 0.9324 discovery AUROC versus 0.8964 for matched supervised logistic regression. External evaluation yielded 0.7778 AUROC on CRRC telemetry (0.7963 with truncation) and 0.7691 on 120-s Figshare triage records. Operating with 2.15~ms execution time under contextual calibration, these results provide an interpretable framework for continuous bedside neuromonitoring.
